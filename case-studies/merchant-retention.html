Merchant retention for a virtual card program

A payments program converts merchants from paper check to a single-use account (SUA) virtual card, and leadership wants one number that says whether it is working. The North Star Metric (NSM): the share of SUA volume retained over a 3-month window. Getting it required turning a duplicate-ridden change log into enrollment history, recovering a churn batch that never hit the log, and modeling marts before the metric, because the warehouse capped every query at 100 GB scanned.

The notebook

The analysis, step by step

Step 1: from a change log to enrollment history

The warehouse records every field change as a row: entity, field, previous value, current value, timestamp. Joining the program is payment_method changing to '12'; churning is a change away from it. Two production facts make this harder than it sounds: the log contains exact duplicate rows (a loader that replays files), and one churn batch was applied by a manual extract-transform-load (ETL) directly in the database, so it never produced log rows at all. Ops kept the list; the query unions it in. Each join is then paired with the merchant’s next event, and open enrollments get the analysis anchor as their fill date.

events → enrollment stints: dedupe, union the off-log batch, pair with LEAD 
WITH method_changes AS (          -- SUA-related rows, deduplicated
    SELECT DISTINCT
        entity_id                  AS merchant_id,
        CAST(change_date AS DATE)  AS change_date,
        val_prev, val_curr
    FROM change_log
    WHERE record_type = 'payment_method'
      AND (val_curr = '12' OR val_prev = '12')
),
events AS (
    SELECT merchant_id, change_date,
           CASE WHEN val_curr = '12' THEN 'join' ELSE 'churn' END AS event
    FROM method_changes
    UNION ALL                      -- the churn batch that never hit the log
    SELECT entity_id, CAST(churn_date AS DATE), 'churn'
    FROM ops_manual_updates
),
ordered AS (
    SELECT *,
           LEAD(change_date) OVER (PARTITION BY merchant_id ORDER BY change_date) AS next_date,
           LEAD(event)       OVER (PARTITION BY merchant_id ORDER BY change_date) AS next_event
    FROM events
)
SELECT merchant_id,
       change_date AS join_date,
       CASE WHEN next_event = 'churn' THEN next_date END AS churn_date,
       COALESCE(CASE WHEN next_event = 'churn' THEN next_date END,
                DATE '2024-01-01') AS filled_churn_date
FROM ordered
WHERE event = 'join'

Step 2: monthly movement is the audit trail

Beginning-of-month (BOM), joined, churned, end-of-month (EOM), on a plain generate_series month spine. The period tells its own story:

Miss the manual batch and every EOM from June on is overstated by 45 merchants; miss the wrongful-conversion wave and the campaign looks like a clean win. The movement table is where those stories get caught.

Step 3: marts before metric

Amazon Athena capped each query at 100 GB scanned, and a year of the raw change log plus payments blew past it. The production fix was dbt: model payment_detail_agg (month × merchant × method, with volume and count) and merchant_retention_record (one row per enrollment stint) once, then let every report and every analyst query the small tables. It also removed the dependency on whoever could re-derive the CDC pairing from memory.

Step 4: the North Star, and what it replaced

The original computed cohort retention in a single pass with Presto map/array algebra, because it could not afford to scan the table twice:

the 2022 version: map_agg, array_intersect, reduce (what aged out) 
1e0 * cardinality(array_intersect(
          map_keys(lag(transaction_volume_M, 3) OVER (ORDER BY M)),
          map_keys(transaction_volume_M))
) / cardinality(map_keys(lag(transaction_volume_M, 3) OVER (ORDER BY M)))
-- ... and reduce(map_values(map_zip_with(...))) to sum retained volume

It worked, and it was unreadable. With marts, the same metric is a self-join anyone can review:

the metric over marts: cohort M-3 joined to month M 
SELECT
    (c.month + INTERVAL 3 MONTH)::DATE                AS month,
    count(*)                                          AS transacted_m3,
    count(t.merchant_id)                              AS still_transacting,
    sum(c.transaction_volume)                         AS volume_m3,
    sum(CASE WHEN t.merchant_id IS NOT NULL
             THEN t.transaction_volume END)           AS volume_retained
FROM transacting c
LEFT JOIN transacting t
  ON t.merchant_id = c.merchant_id
 AND t.month = (c.month + INTERVAL 3 MONTH)::DATE
GROUP BY 1

Step 5: two lenses, one honest answer

Retention has to be asked two ways. The activity lens (transacted in M−3 and in M) answers “is the volume repeating?”; the enrollment lens (still on the payment method) answers “did they leave the program?”. Conflate them and occasionally paid merchants look like churn:

SegmentEnrollment retentionActivity retentionRead
occasional92.0%51.1%keep the method (interchange barely registers), just skip months
regular85.4%85.6%lenses agree: they transact every month
high_frequency65.3%65.9%revert the most: interchange and reconciliation costs compound weekly

That asymmetry is why the North Star divides by the transactable (enrollment) denominator, and why volume retention (73%) runs below merchant retention: the heaviest payers churn the method hardest. Total volume still grows through all of it, acquisition outpacing churn, which is the conclusion the original dashboard existed to defend.
